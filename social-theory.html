Theoretical Frameworks

The sociology behind the initiative

← Back to Dashboard

To effectively empower women in sports, we must understand the societal structures that currently limit participation. This project relies on three key sociological frameworks to guide our implementation policies.

Intersectionality

Kimberlé Crenshaw

Addressing Multiple Barriers

Women do not experience discrimination in a vacuum. A woman’s experience in sport is shaped by the overlapping barriers of gender, race, class, and ability.

Application: Our marketing ensures representation of diverse ethnicities and body types, and our costings include subsidies for low-income participants.

Self-Determination

Ryan & Deci

Autonomy, Competence, Relatedness

Motivation thrives when individuals feel they have a choice (Autonomy), feel effective (Competence), and feel connected to others (Relatedness).

Application: We move away from strict "drill-sergeant" coaching styles to cooperative, social fitness sessions that prioritize fun over competition.

Social Model of Disability

Oliver

Removing Environmental Barriers

It is not the individual's impairment that disables them, but the barriers in society (e.g., stairs without ramps, exclusionary rules).

Application: All selected venues are wheelchair accessible, and activities are adaptive by design (e.g., seated volleyball options).
